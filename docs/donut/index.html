A piece is not a weight

Every number above depends on what counts as one donut, and USDA's own portion table runs from 13 g to 157 g — a hole against a jumbo, the same food either way. Each dot is a portion USDA lists; the bar spans them. This is the reason a donut's calorie count is an argument about size.

Beignet

A deep-fried pastry of French origin, in New Orleans a square of dough fried until it puffs hollow and buried in confectioner's sugar. Beignet means bump.

Bismarck

A round raised donut with no hole, fried, then injected with fruit preserve, custard or cream and finished in sugar or icing.

Buttermilk bar

Cake batter cut in a bar rather than a ring, fried so it splits open along the top, and glazed heavily enough that the glaze runs into the split.

Cake donut

A ring of thick batter, not dough, raised by baking powder or soda rather than yeast and dropped straight into the fat from a depositor.

Chocolate cake

A cake ring with cocoa in the batter, glazed or sugared. Dark, dense, and sweeter than the plain cake ring on the published numbers.

Cider donut

An American cake donut flavoured with apple cider, cinnamon and nutmeg in the batter, usually rolled in cinnamon or granulated sugar.

Donut holes

The centres cut out of ring donuts, fried and sold by the bag — or, more often now, made on purpose from the same dough or batter and never cut from anything.

Fastnacht

A Pennsylvania Dutch fried donut, often made with potato dough, eaten in the days of Carnival and above all on Shrove Tuesday.

Numbers come from USDA FoodData Central's SR Legacy set, which publishes nine generic doughnut foods per 100 grams with its own portion weights; they are public domain and each row links to the food it came from.
