a long oneWhy the egg rolls

also: the second menu · donuts and Chinese food

A donut shop that also sells egg rolls, or breakfast burritos, or bacon egg and cheese, or kolaches, is not being eccentric. It is doing arithmetic out loud. The people running those shops have said so in print, repeatedly, and this page collects what they said.

The answer, from the people doing it

Jolly Chan opened China Express and Donut by the 24th Street BART station in San Francisco in 1993. He had come from Cambodia in 1981. Asked by KQED in 2021 why the two menus sit together, he said the combination "came out of necessity" — "We have to sell more stuff to make up the rent and the expense." His customer base, he said, was 80 percent Latino.

That is the whole mechanism. A donut shop's trade is over by ten in the morning. The rent is not.

Who was in the shops

Ted Ngoy, a Cambodian refugee who reached Camp Pendleton in 1975, trained at Winchell's, bought Christy's Donuts in La Habra in 1977, and then leased shops to relatives and to refugee families he sponsored — more than a hundred, by his own account. KQED reported that roughly 90 percent of California's 5,000-plus independent donut shops were Cambodian-owned.

A trade that arrives through one community carries that community's other food with it, and a fryer that is already hot does not care what goes in it.

The same arithmetic, different accents

The pattern repeats wherever an early-morning counter needs a second product:

— Southern California: egg rolls, chow mein and fried rice in a warmer beside the case. — Texas: kolaches, and above all klobasniky — sausage in kolache dough — which means the shop runs an oven as well as a kettle. — New Mexico and the Southwest: breakfast burritos, wrapped in foil, red or green. — New York: the bacon, egg and cheese on a kaiser roll, at a deli or bodega where the donuts are bought in. — The South: biscuits and gravy. — The 2020s: boba, and Korean corn dogs beside mochi donuts.

Inference — the second menu is the most reliable sign that a shop is owned by the people working in it. A company-owned store sells what the company prints.

Why it gets read wrong

The combination gets written up as a quirk — as a joke about America, or a listicle item. The shops are describing a cost structure. Dorothy Chow, whose father built B & H Bakery Distributors after surviving the Cambodian genocide, told KQED he started it "to help our own people": the supply chain behind these shops was built by the same community that runs them.

This project tags what a shop sells beside its donuts, names where the tag came from, and leaves the quirk to somebody else.
